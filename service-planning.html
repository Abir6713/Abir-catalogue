Topic & Content Planning

Starting at ₦50,000

What It Entails

Every good textbook starts before the first sentence is written. Content planning is the stage where we define what the book is about, who it's for, and how it will be structured — chapter by chapter.

Without this step, authors often write themselves into corners: chapters overlap, key topics get missed, and the book loses its thread by Chapter 5. We build the map first, so writing becomes a straight road instead of a maze.

What's Included

  • Audience definition: Who exactly is this for — first-year students, professionals, secondary school pupils?
  • Scope definition: What's in, what's out, and what's left for a second volume.
  • Chapter breakdown: A full table of contents with titles, subtopics and word-count targets.
  • Learning outcomes: What should a reader know by the end of each chapter?
  • Sequencing logic: Arranging chapters so each one builds on the last.
  • Curriculum alignment: Matching the structure to national or institutional standards where relevant.
  • Delivery document: A written plan you can hand to any writer and get consistent results.

Sample: How It Works

Here is a real chapter-planning document we delivered for a Financial Accounting textbook. Each chapter has a clear focus and target length.

Sample Plan — Financial Accounting for Nigerian Students
Chapter 1 — The Nature of Accounting (3,500 words)
  • Definition and purpose of accounting
  • Users of financial information
  • The accounting equation
  • Learning outcome: Students can explain why accounting matters and identify its users.
Chapter 2 — Double-Entry Principles (4,200 words)
  • Debits and credits defined
  • Rules for each account class
  • Worked examples with Nigerian currency
  • Learning outcome: Students can correctly record any basic transaction.
Chapter 3 — Books of Original Entry (3,800 words)
  • Journals and daybooks
  • Posting to the ledger
  • Practice exercises
  • Learning outcome: Students can move transactions from source documents to ledger.
